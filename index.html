Primary Care for Cancers

An interactive, evidence-checked learning resource for general practitioners, family medicine specialists and trainees in Malaysia — and for students, nurses, pharmacists and allied health professionals. It is organised around what primary care does: recognise, refer, share care, follow up, support and safety-net.

Part A · Understand

The burden of cancer worldwide and in Malaysia, how cancer is classified and staged, and what causes it.

1Module 1: Cancer patterns in the world and in Malaysia, and how cancer is staged

Know the global and local burden; read stage and grade; understand late presentation; plan for equity.

You will be able to:

  • Describe global and Malaysian cancer patterns — leading cancers by sex, regional and development-related variation, survival gaps and projections — using GLOBOCAN 2024 and the Malaysia National Cancer Registry correctly.
  • Interpret crude and age-standardised rates, cumulative (0–74 year) risk, mortality-to-incidence ratios and five-year prevalence, and tell registry counts from modelled estimates.
  • Explain the principles common to all cancer staging: T, N and M; the c, p, y, r and a prefixes; stage groups; non-anatomic prognostic factors; and the difference between stage and grade.

Interactive: Statistics decoder; Practice-population estimator (an estimate, not a prediction); Staging explorer; Recognise-and-act drill.

Cases: Puan Aminah, Encik Hassan.

2Module 2: What causes cancer: risk factors and carcinogens

Explain causes; identify modifiable risks; counsel on hazards.

You will be able to:

  • Explain cancer causation as a mix of inherited, environmental and chance factors, in words patients understand.
  • Interpret International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) groups as the strength of evidence that something can cause cancer (hazard), not the size of the risk.
  • Identify the modifiable causes most relevant to Malaysian patients — tobacco, betel quid, alcohol, infections, salted fish, aflatoxins and excess body fat — and ask about them in routine care.

Interactive: IARC agent lookup.

Part B · Prevent and detect early

Inherited risk, prevention in everyday consultations, and screening programmes — Malaysian guidance first.

3Module 3: Hereditary cancer risk and genetics referral

Identify inherited risk; refer for genetics; coordinate risk management.

You will be able to:

  • Take and record a three-generation cancer family history, on both the maternal and paternal sides.
  • Recognise pedigree patterns that suggest hereditary breast and ovarian cancer, Lynch syndrome, polyposis and rarer syndromes.
  • Apply referral criteria — the Malaysian CPGs and NICE side by side — to decide who needs genetics assessment.

Interactive: Family cancer history record; Breast and ovarian family-history referral checker; Bowel cancer family and Lynch syndrome referral checker; What does this result mean? and more.

Cases: Ms Lee, Encik Rahman.

About 40–50 minutes · 9-question quiz · 28 sources

4Module 4: Prevention in the consultation

Give brief prevention advice; vaccinate; avoid ineffective chemoprevention.

You will be able to:

  • Deliver brief, evidence-based advice on tobacco (including betel quid and vaping), alcohol, body weight, diet and physical activity, and choose the one change that matters most for each patient.
  • Advise on HPV and hepatitis B vaccination using the Malaysian National Immunisation Programme first, and explain where WHO and US positions differ.
  • Identify who should be tested and treated for Helicobacter pylori to lower gastric cancer risk, following the Malaysian consensus.

Interactive: HPV vaccination eligibility card; Prevention priorities: profile to top three actions; Agree a prevention plan with the patient.

Cases: Encik Faizal, Puan Kavitha.

5Module 5: Screening and early detection

Offer screening; support shared decisions; act on results.

You will be able to:

  • Apply the principles of screening to explain test-specific benefits and harms, including lead-time bias and overdiagnosis.
  • Determine screening eligibility for cervical, breast, colorectal and lung cancer using Malaysian guidance first, and compare WHO, USPSTF, ACS and UK/EU positions side by side.
  • Use absolute numbers and icon arrays to support shared decisions about PSA testing, lung screening and mammography at 40–49.

Interactive: Benefit–harm explorer: what happens to 1000 people; Cervical screening checker; Breast screening checker; Colorectal screening checker and more.

Cases: Madam Wong, Mr Kumar.

Part C · Recognise and refer

Recognising possible cancer, referring at the right speed, safety-netting, and the conversations that follow.

6Module 6: Recognising possible cancer and referring

Recognise; investigate; refer at the right speed; safety-net.

You will be able to:

  • Recognise symptoms, signs and test results that call for investigation or referral for suspected cancer, using Malaysian CPG criteria first with NICE NG12 and SRG 2025 alongside.
  • Choose an appropriate first investigation in primary care and the right urgency of referral, and explain where Malaysian and international timeframes differ.
  • Identify the cancers most often missed or diagnosed late in primary care, and the patient, clinician, test and system reasons behind them.

Interactive: Positive-predictive-value ladder: make your estimate first; Missed-cancer explorer; Symptom-to-referral navigator; Safety-net builder and more.

Cases: Mr Lim, Aisyah, Madam Kaur.

7Module 7: Breaking bad news and the referral conversation

Break bad news; prepare for referral; support at diagnosis.

You will be able to:

  • Prepare for and hold a breaking-bad-news conversation using a structured, culturally responsive approach (SPIKES adapted to Malaysian preferences).
  • Respond to a family's request not to tell the patient in a way that respects the patient's autonomy and the Malaysian Medical Council consent guideline (2025).
  • Give patients being referred the practical information they need, using a printable referral-conversation checklist.

Interactive: Referral-conversation checklist (printable).

Part D · Share care during treatment

What cancer treatments do, how to triage side-effects, and how to recognise and act on emergencies.

8Module 8: Cancer treatments: what primary care needs to know

Understand treatments; manage medicines and comorbidity; discuss CAM.

You will be able to:

  • Explain the main types and intents of cancer treatment to patients in plain language, including adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapy.
  • Decode common terms in oncology letters (biomarkers, performance status, regimens, response) and act on what they imply for primary care.
  • Anticipate how cancer treatment affects other conditions and medicines, and check for drug–drug, herb–drug and food interactions.

Interactive: Decode the oncology letter; CAM and supplement record to share with the oncology team; Vaccination timing checker.

Cases: Puan Siti, Mr Lim.

About 40–50 minutes · 10-question quiz · 55 sources

9Module 9: Side-effects and toxicity triage

Triage side-effects; manage mild toxicity; escalate early.

You will be able to:

  • Triage symptoms in a person on systemic anticancer therapy using CTCAE grades and a red–amber–green approach.
  • Manage common mild side-effects in primary care — nausea, mouth, bowel, skin, fatigue, appetite and neuropathy — and recognise when to escalate.
  • Recognise immune-related adverse events by organ, order the right first tests, and contact oncology the same day.

Interactive: Make a treatment alert and safety-net card; Triage cards: symptom → grade → action → who to call; irAE organ map.

Case: Mr Raju.

10Module 10: Oncologic emergencies: recognise and act

Recognise emergencies; act first; refer fast.

You will be able to:

  • Recognise the presenting features of the main oncologic emergencies in primary care, including atypical presentations such as neutropenic sepsis without fever.
  • Take safe immediate actions in the clinic before transfer.
  • Arrange the right level and speed of referral, using Malaysian services where they exist.

Part E · Follow up and support

Long-term follow-up and shared care, psychosocial and spiritual support, older adults, and end-of-life care.

11Module 11: Survivorship and shared follow-up care

Share follow-up care; manage late effects; promote recovery and return to life.

You will be able to:

  • Describe shared-care models and agree roles with oncology teams, using a Malaysian clinic as the setting.
  • Use a treatment summary and survivorship care plan to organise follow-up, naming the guideline behind each surveillance schedule.
  • Recognise and manage common late and long-term effects by treatment exposure, including cardiovascular and bone health.

Interactive: Survivorship care-plan builder (printable); Late-effects checker by treatment exposure; Late-effects triage cards; Exercise-prescription tool (FITT).

Cases: Madam Chong, Encik Azman.

12Module 12: Psychosocial and spiritual care

Screen distress; support mental health; respect spiritual needs; support carers.

You will be able to:

  • Screen for distress at key points of the cancer journey and identify probable depression and anxiety using tools validated in Malaysia.
  • Offer stepped psychosocial care matched to severity, and refer urgently when there is risk of self-harm or severe illness.
  • Recognise fear of cancer recurrence, respond with a brief structured conversation and refer when it is clinically significant.

Interactive: Distress-screening practice: interpret scores side by side; Stepped-care navigator; My plan for the weeks before a scan; Spiritual-history practice prompts.

13Module 13: Cancer in older adults and multimorbidity

Assess older adults; manage multimorbidity; align care with goals.

You will be able to:

  • Use a brief screening tool, the G8, to identify older adults with cancer who need a geriatric assessment, and explain what an abnormal score means.
  • Contribute geriatric-assessment information — function, falls, cognition, mood, nutrition, comorbidity, medicines and social support — to the treatment decisions made by surgical and oncology teams.
  • Review and deprescribe medicines safely in an older adult with multimorbidity before and during cancer treatment, using Malaysian and international criteria.

Interactive: G8 screening calculator (educational); Domain navigator: tool, threshold and primary-care action; Geriatric summary for the surgical or oncology team.

Cases: Encik Abdullah, Deprescribing review.

14Module 14: Palliative and end-of-life care in primary care

Identify palliative needs early; control symptoms; plan end-of-life care at home.

You will be able to:

  • Identify patients who would benefit from palliative care on the basis of needs rather than prognosis, using structured prompts.
  • Begin and document an advance care planning (ACP) conversation in line with Ministry of Health Malaysia (MOH) guidance, including the role of a healthcare proxy.
  • Assess cancer pain and start, titrate and review oral opioids safely, with breakthrough doses and constipation prevention.

Interactive: Palliative-needs checker (case-based); ACP conversation guide; Worked examples (oral morphine); Symptom cards and more.
